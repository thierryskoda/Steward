# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Developers who work across several AI-assisted codebases and need a quick, trustworthy way to monitor project quality. They use Steward during normal development to understand findings, control scanning, and approve consequential changes without leaving their local environment.

## Product Purpose

Steward continuously compares each selected project with its own engineering rules and context. It turns drift into focused, evidence-backed findings, keeps ambiguous changes under human control, and makes ongoing maintenance practical across many repositories. Success means the user can see what needs attention, understand why, and act without manually re-auditing every project.

## Brand Personality

Calm, rigorous, and direct. Steward should feel like a dependable technical companion: specific about state and evidence, restrained in presentation, and confident without sounding absolute.

## Anti-references

- Glossy generic SaaS dashboards that decorate simple status with oversized metrics or excessive cards.
- Opaque autonomous-agent interfaces that hide what is running, what changed, or what still needs human approval.
- Dense operations consoles that make routine project health difficult to scan.
- Playful copy or motion that weakens trust in technical evidence and workflow state.

## Design Principles

- Make current state and the next safe action immediately understandable.
- Preserve human control at consequential decision points.
- Prefer compact evidence and project-specific language over generic AI claims.
- Keep routine clean outcomes quiet; give failures and blocked work actionable prominence.
- Reuse established interaction patterns so the user can move between projects without relearning the interface.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. All controls need keyboard access, visible focus, semantic labels, and status meaning that does not depend on color alone. Text and interactive states must maintain sufficient contrast in light and dark themes. Respect reduced-motion preferences and keep operational language concise and literal.
